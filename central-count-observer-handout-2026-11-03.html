The five things that matter most

  1. Watch and write. Don’t argue. Your job is a precise, time-stamped record. Whether something was a violation gets decided later, from your notes. Record what you saw, not what you concluded. TEAM
  2. Talk only to the designated election official or the officials named for you at check-in. Don’t talk to workers at the tables. EL 4.04(4), (14) LAW
  3. Hands off. Never touch a ballot, envelope, container, return, or machine. § 5.87(1); EL 4.03(9) LAW
  4. Photos are allowed at central count, with three exceptions: no passwords or log-ins, no confidential information (voted ballots, dates of birth, ID numbers), and no voters dropping off or fixing a ballot. EL 4.05(6)(b), 4.03(10), 4.05(3)(b) LAW APPLIED
  5. Anything major: find your team lead right away. That includes ballots accepted after 8 p.m., single-party teams handling ballots, broken or missing seals, or a results export that looks wrong. TEAM

Arrival & check-in

  • Tell the designated election official you are there to observe, and sign the observer log. Print your full name, street address, municipality, and organization (if any). Signing confirms you understand the rules and will follow them. EL 4.04(1); § 7.41(1) LAW
  • Show photo ID with your name. It does not have to be a voter-ID-qualifying document. EL 4.04(1) LAW
  • The official must offer you a summary of the rules and tell you three things: (1) who answers your questions, (2) how you can move between observation areas, and (3) where ballots are remade. If they don’t, ask. EL 4.03(2)(b) LAW
  • Get your observer badge and wear it the whole time. EL 4.03(2)(c) LAW
  • Check in with your team lead and get your assigned area. TEAM
  • Write down your arrival time, on the observer form or as a time-stamped screenshot. TEAM
  • Ask the official for the redacted results-download instructions. MEC said observers and media can have a copy. REPORTED

What to bring / wear

  • Photo ID · clipboard or folder · several pens · observer forms and tabulator forms · water and snacks · phone charger. TEAM
  • Comfortable shoes. You may be standing for hours.
  • No backpacks or large purses. A satchel about the size of a ballot envelope is fine. TEAM
  • No campaign gear of any kind. Nothing showing a candidate, party, or referendum position on the ballot, and nothing that suggests you are an election official. EL 4.04(10) LAW
Who may not observe: a candidate on the ballot at that location. Anyone else may observe, and you don’t have to represent an organization. § 7.41(1); EL 4.02(10), 4.04(1) LAW

Your rights as an observer (what officials must provide)

RightSource
To observe all counting of ballots at the central counting location.EL 4.05(6)(a) LAW

Do

  • Follow the designated official’s instructions. Refusing can get you removed. EL 4.04(2) LAW
  • Ask questions only of the official(s) named at check-in. EL 4.04(4) LAW
  • Keep conversation short and quiet. EL 4.04(8) LAW
  • Move between observation areas only in the way the official has set. EL 4.04(3) LAW
  • Tell another observer before you step away so your area stays covered. TEAM
  • Be courteous to everyone, all the time. Most workers are doing their best. Treat them as allies in a good process. TEAM
  • Record audio only of conversations you are part of. Wisconsin is a one-party-consent state. § 968.31(2)(c) LAW Record openly. Don’t hide devices. TEAM
  • Hand your notes to your team lead before you leave, and write down your departure time. TEAM

Don’t

  • Touch any ballot, envelope, container, return, or equipment. § 5.87(1); EL 4.03(9) LAW
  • Look at confidential information: DL or state ID numbers, SSN digits, dates of birth, confidential-voter data, guardianship information, or a voted ballot. EL 4.02(5), 4.03(10) LAW
  • Photograph or record passwords or log-ins on tabulators or computers. TEAM
  • Photograph or record a person returning an absentee ballot or fixing a certificate envelope. Voters drop ballots off here all day. EL 4.05(3)(b) LAW APPLIED: this site is both a central count and a meeting of the absentee board, so we apply the stricter rule.
  • Be loud or disruptive. EL 4.04(6) LAW
  • Electioneer, distribute election material, or discuss any candidate, party, or question on the ballot. EL 4.04(9), (11) LAW
  • Start a conversation with a voter. If one speaks to you, keep it brief, say you’re an observer and not an official, and point them to an official. EL 4.04(13) LAW
  • Talk to unfamiliar observers or to the press. TEAM
  • Challenge a ballot without your team lead. See “Challenges.” TEAM

B. Staffing — bipartisan teams

At central count, workers (other than specially trained technicians) must be split equally between the two major parties. Duties must be done by teams with equal numbers from each party whenever enough people from each party are available. § 5.86(1) LAW Write down workers’ wristband colors and names if you can. TEAM A pending complaint alleges that one official exported results in August without an official from the other party present. Watch who does each export. REPORTED

D. Opening & handling ballots

  • The board checks that each ballot carries the issuing clerk’s endorsement (initials). § 7.52(3)(a) LAW
  • The board writes each voter’s poll list number on the back of the ballot. The law currently requires this, and it is a known ballot-secrecy concern. Never photograph a numbered ballot back next to the poll list. § 7.52(3)(a) LAW APPLIED
  • Write down the time you see ballots being distributed to tables, and who brought them. TEAM
  • The number of ballots must reconcile with the poll list. If there are extra ballots, they are drawn out at random and set aside. Blank ballots are marked, laid aside, and kept. § 7.52(4) LAW

E. Remakes (duplicate ballots)

  • A ballot the tabulator can’t read is duplicated in the presence of witnesses. The original is labeled “Damaged Ballot” and the copy “Duplicate Damaged Ballot,” and both carry the same serial number. Overvoted ballots follow the same pattern (“Overvoted” / “Duplicate Overvoted”). § 5.85(2)(b), (3) LAW
  • Check that two inspectors from different parties do every remake. Record how many remakes there were, the reason for each, and whether the serial numbers match. § 5.86(1) LAW TEAM
  • The remake table must be within 3 to 8 ft of an observation area. EL 4.03(1)(b)3 LAW

F. Closing the count & exporting results (highest-risk step)

  • August 2026 failure: on 5 of 9 tabulators, audit logs were exported instead of results, covering about 15,000 absentee ballots. MEC has since adopted laminated, step-by-step instructions with photos for the export teams. REPORTED
  • For each tabulator, record: tabulator #, export start and end times, who performed it (party/wristband), whether a second-party official watched, and whether the team followed the posted steps. TEAM
  • Keep your eyes on the data sticks, without filming passwords: when they were sealed or bagged, who carried them, and when they left with the escort. TEAM
  • By closing time, the clerk must post how many absentee ballots were mailed or sent out and how many came back. Look for it. § 7.52(1)(c) LAW

Live issues for this election

1. Areas you can’t see (the “Secure Ballot Room”)

Observers have reported over several elections that rooms where envelopes are stored, sorted, or scanned are walled off or have covered windows. REPORTED WEC dismissed one complaint about this 5–1 on May 7, 2026. A newer complaint from the August primary is pending. REPORTED Don’t try to get in, and don’t argue. Record it as a coverage gap: the time; which activity you could not see; where it happened; what you were told, by whom; and whether the official is recording the reason under EL 4.03(5). § 7.41(2); EL 4.05(6)(a), 4.03(5) LAW

2. Ballot spoiling

A returned absentee ballot cannot be spoiled and replaced, even if the voter changed their mind or a candidate dropped out. A voter who has not yet returned a ballot and made a mistake can still get a replacement. Returning a ballot so a defective certificate can be fixed is a separate, permitted process. WEC adopted this guidance July 9, 2026, and a Dane County court upheld it July 29, 2026 (Bins v. WEC, 2026CV002400). If you see an attempt to spoil or replace a returned ballot, record it and tell your lead. § 6.86(5), 6.87(9) LAW REPORTED (Appeals are possible. Your lead will confirm the status Tuesday morning.)

3. Law enforcement & federal personnel

WEC guidance (Oct 15, 2026) says that law enforcement generally should not be present unless they are voting, doing a legitimate law-enforcement action, or responding to an official’s request. Federal agency representatives may observe if they give proper documentation, and they are bound by the same observer rules as you. MEC says armed federal officers are prohibited at polling places and at Central Count. REPORTED Your role: don’t engage. Record the time, the agency (if shown), whether they were uniformed or armed, what they did, and how officials responded. Tell your lead immediately.

4. What has not changed

  • No Monday pre-processing. Ballots still can’t be opened before 7 a.m. on Election Day. The 2025–26 bills to change this (and to end central count) did not become law; SB 624 failed March 23, 2026. REPORTED
  • Ch. EL 4 (the observer rule) took effect Aug 1, 2025 and governs this election. LAW

If something looks wrong

  1. Write it down first: time, table/tabulator #, ward, voter or poll number if it was announced, workers’ names or wristband colors, exactly what happened.
  2. Was it noted on the EL-104 (Inspectors’ Statement)? Write yes, no, or unknown.
  3. Major issue: find your team lead right away. If you can’t reach them, call or text the hotline. TEAM
  4. If your lead tells you to, ask the designated official, calmly, to note it on the EL-104. Then write down the answer you got.
  5. Can’t get a name? Step back and photograph the official discreetly, never voters or ballots. TEAM

If you are warned or told to leave

  1. Stop the conduct you were warned about. You get one warning. EL 4.03(11) LAW
  2. If you are ordered to leave, comply. Politely ask for the written order with the reason and signature, and for the opposite-party official to be offered the chance to sign it. EL 4.03(11)(b)–(c) LAW
  3. Write down the time, names, and the exact words used. The incident must be reported to WEC within 60 days. EL 4.03(11)(d) LAW
  4. Call your team lead from outside.
Challenges: a Wisconsin elector may challenge an absentee ballot for cause, under oath. § 7.52(5); EL 4.04(5); ch. EL 9 LAW Never challenge on your own. It goes through your team lead. TEAM

For team leads only — complaint routes. Observers just hand in notes. A § 5.06 complaint against a local election official may be filed only by an elector of the jurisdiction (here, City of Milwaukee voters). It must be sworn and filed promptly, and the remedy is a WEC order. A § 5.05 sworn complaint (form EL-1100) can be filed by anyone for alleged violations of chs. 5–10 or 12, and can lead to a referral to the district attorney.
